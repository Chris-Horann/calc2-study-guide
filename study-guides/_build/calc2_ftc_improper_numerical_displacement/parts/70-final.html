1. For $\displaystyle\int\frac{(\ln t)^{2/5}}{3t}\,dt$ (notes p. 22), the first move is…
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{x^2}{(x^3+5)^4}\,dx.$
$u=x^3+5$, $du=3x^2\,dx$.
$\tfrac13\int u^{-4}\,du=\tfrac13\cdot\frac{u^{-3}}{-3}+C=-\frac{1}{9(x^3+5)^3}+C$.
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{2x+1}}\,dx.$
$u=2x+1$, so $du=2\,dx$, and the stray $x$ is $\frac{u-1}{2}$ (the notes p. 6 move).
$\int\frac{(u-1)/2}{\sqrt u}\cdot\frac{du}{2}=\frac14\int\big(u^{1/2}-u^{-1/2}\big)\,du$.
$\frac14\Big(\frac23u^{3/2}-2u^{1/2}\Big)+C=\frac16(2x+1)^{3/2}-\frac12(2x+1)^{1/2}+C$.
4. For $\displaystyle\int x^2\ln x\,dx$, choose $u$:
Find $\displaystyle\int x\cos(3x)\,dx.$
$u=x$, $dv=\cos3x\,dx$: $du=dx$, $v=\frac13\sin3x$.
$\frac x3\sin3x-\frac13\int\sin3x\,dx=\frac x3\sin3x+\frac19\cos3x+C$.
Find $\displaystyle\int x^3e^{x^2}\,dx.$
Substitute first: $w=x^2$, $dw=2x\,dx$, and $x^3\,dx=x^2\cdot x\,dx=\tfrac12w\,dw$.
$\tfrac12\int we^w\,dw$: parts with $u=w$.
$\tfrac12(we^w-e^w)+C=\tfrac12e^{x^2}(x^2-1)+C$. The same pattern as notes pp. 12 and 24: substitution exposes the product.
7. Which template fits $\dfrac{3x+1}{(x-1)^2(x^2+9)}$?
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{5u^2-3u-29}{(u+3)(u-2)^2}\,du.$
Template $\frac{A}{u+3}+\frac{B}{u-2}+\frac{C}{(u-2)^2}$. You should find $A=1$, $B=4$, $C=-3$.
$\int\Big(\frac{1}{u+3}+\frac{4}{u-2}-\frac{3}{(u-2)^2}\Big)du=\ln|u+3|+4\ln|u-2|+\frac{3}{u-2}+C$.
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{2w^3+6w^2-4w+11}{w^2+3w-4}\,dw.$
Degree 3 ≥ 2: divide. The quotient is $2w$ and the remainder $4w+11$.
$\frac{4w+11}{(w+4)(w-1)}=\frac{A}{w+4}+\frac{B}{w-1}$, with $A=1$ and $B=3$.
$\int\Big(2w+\frac{1}{w+4}+\frac{3}{w-1}\Big)dw=w^2+\ln|w+4|+3\ln|w-1|+C$.
10. $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}x^3\,dx=0$. What does that tell you?
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^4|x-1|\,dx.$
Split at $x=1$.
$\int_0^1(1-x)\,dx+\int_1^4(x-1)\,dx=\tfrac12+\tfrac92=5$ (or add the areas of two triangles).
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}\left|\sin x-\cos x\right|\,dx.$
$\sin x=\cos x$ at $x=\frac\pi4$ in $[0,\pi]$. Which is bigger before that point?
$\int_0^{\pi/4}(\cos x-\sin x)\,dx+\int_{\pi/4}^{\pi}(\sin x-\cos x)\,dx$.
$\big[\sin x+\cos x\big]_0^{\pi/4}=\sqrt2-1$ and $\big[-\cos x-\sin x\big]_{\pi/4}^{\pi}=1+\sqrt2$. The total is $2\sqrt2\approx2.828$.
13. How should you set up $\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(x+1)\sqrt x}$?
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_4^{\infty}x^{-3/2}\,dx$ or show that it diverges.
An antiderivative is $-2x^{-1/2}$.
$\lim_{b\to\infty}\left(-\frac{2}{\sqrt b}\right)+\frac2{\sqrt4}=1.$
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(x+1)\sqrt x}$ or show that it diverges.
Try $u=\sqrt x$, so $x=u^2$ and $dx=2u\,du$. What does the integrand become?
$\int\frac{2u\,du}{(u^2+1)u}=2\int\frac{du}{1+u^2}=2\tan^{-1}\sqrt x$. Now handle both problem points.
$\int_0^1=2\tan^{-1}1-\lim_{a\to0^+}2\tan^{-1}\sqrt a=\frac\pi2$ and $\int_1^\infty=\lim_{b\to\infty}2\tan^{-1}\sqrt b-\frac\pi2=\pi-\frac\pi2=\frac\pi2$. Total: $\pi$.
16. The Trapezoid coefficients for $n=5$ are…
Compute the Midpoint Rule estimate $M_2$ for $\displaystyle\int_0^2x^2\,dx$. Is it too big or too small?
$\Delta x=1$, and the midpoints are $\frac12$ and $\frac32$.
$1\cdot\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac94\big)=\tfrac52=2.5$, too small (the exact value is $\tfrac83$). For a concave-up curve the Midpoint Rule underestimates, while the Trapezoid Rule ($T_2=3$) overestimates.

19 · Explain why (§2.6)

Simpson's Rule with $n=2$ gives exactly $\frac83$ for $\int_0^2x^2\,dx$. Why is it exact here, when it's only approximate for $e^{-x^2}$?

A model explanation

Simpson's Rule replaces the curve on each pair of strips with the parabola through three of its points. If the curve is a parabola, like $x^2$, the “approximating” parabola is the curve itself, so the areas are identical and the error is 0. $e^{-x^2}$ isn't a parabola, so the fitted parabolas only nearly match it. (The textbook adds a surprise in Remark 2.6.12: Simpson's Rule is exact even for cubics.)

$v(t)=t^2-1$ m/s on $[0,2]$. Find the total distance traveled.

A particle has velocity $v(t)=\dfrac{2t}{(1+t^2)^2}$ for $t\ge0$. What total distance does it travel over $[0,\infty)$?
$v\ge0$, so distance = $\int_0^\infty v\,dt$. Substitute $u=1+t^2$.
An antiderivative is $-\frac{1}{1+t^2}$, so $\lim_{b\to\infty}\left(-\frac1{1+b^2}\right)+1=1.$
22. A particle has constant velocity $\vec v(t)=(4,3)$ for $0\le t\le2$. Its distance traveled and the length of its displacement are…
A particle has position $\vec p(t)=\big(t^3,\ \tfrac32t^2\big)$ for $0\le t\le2$. Find the total distance traveled.
$\vec v=(3t^2,\ 3t)$, so $|\vec v|=\sqrt{9t^4+9t^2}=3t\sqrt{t^2+1}$.
$\int_0^23t\sqrt{t^2+1}\,dt=\big[(t^2+1)^{3/2}\big]_0^2=5^{3/2}-1=5\sqrt5-1\approx10.180$.
A particle has velocity $\vec v(t)=\big(e^{-t},\ e^{-t}\big)$ for $t\ge0$. What total distance does it travel over $[0,\infty)$?
$|\vec v|=\sqrt{2e^{-2t}}=\sqrt2\,e^{-t}$.
$\int_0^\infty\sqrt2\,e^{-t}\,dt=\lim_{b\to\infty}\sqrt2\big(1-e^{-b}\big)$ (§2.5, Type I).
$\sqrt2\approx1.414$. The displacement tends to $(1,1)$, also of length $\sqrt2$: the particle moves along a straight line in one direction, so the two agree.
25. Which integral gives the length of the curve $y=e^x$ from $x=0$ to $x=1$?
Find the length of the curve $y=\tfrac23(x-1)^{3/2}$ for $1\le x\le4$.
$y'=(x-1)^{1/2}$, so $1+y'^2=x$.
$\int_1^4\sqrt x\,dx=\tfrac23\big(8-1\big)=\tfrac{14}{3}\approx4.667$.
Find the length of the curve $(\cos^3t,\ \sin^3t)$ for $0\le t\le\frac\pi2$ (a quarter of an astroid).
$x'=-3\cos^2t\sin t$ and $y'=3\sin^2t\cos t$.
$x'^2+y'^2=9\sin^2t\cos^2t\,(\cos^2t+\sin^2t)$, so the speed is $3\sin t\cos t$ on $[0,\frac\pi2]$.
$\int_0^{\pi/2}3\sin t\cos t\,dt=\big[\tfrac32\sin^2t\big]_0^{\pi/2}=\tfrac32$ (substitute $u=\sin t$). The chord from $(1,0)$ to $(0,1)$ is $\sqrt2\approx1.414<1.5$ ✓.
28. The region between $y=\sqrt x$ and $y=\frac x2$ ($0\le x\le4$) is spun about the $x$-axis. The area of the slice at $x$ is…
The region bounded by $y=\sqrt x$, $y=0$ and $x=4$ is spun about the line $y=2$. Find the volume.
The axis $y=2$ lies above the region (which reaches up to $y=2$ only at $x=4$). Which edge is farther from it?
$R=2-0=2$ (to the $x$-axis) and $r=2-\sqrt x$, so $A(x)=\pi\big(4-(2-\sqrt x)^2\big)=\pi\big(4\sqrt x-x\big)$.
$\pi\int_0^4\big(4\sqrt x-x\big)\,dx=\pi\big(\tfrac83\cdot8-8\big)=\frac{40\pi}{3}\approx41.89$.
The region under $y=x^{-3/4}$ for $x\ge1$ is spun about the $x$-axis. Is the volume finite? If so, find it.
The slices are disks: $A(x)=\pi\big(x^{-3/4}\big)^2=\pi x^{-3/2}$.
$\pi\int_1^\infty x^{-3/2}\,dx$. Compare notes p. 29.
$\pi\lim_{b\to\infty}\big[-2x^{-1/2}\big]_1^b=2\pi\approx6.283$. The surprise: the flat region itself has infinite area, since $\int_1^\infty x^{-3/4}\,dx$ diverges (like notes p. 32's $x^{-0.8}$). Yet the solid has finite volume, because squaring the radius makes it shrink fast enough.
