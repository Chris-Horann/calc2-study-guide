Stage 1 · Learn

The big idea

Big idea

Washers slice a solid perpendicular to the axis of rotation. Shells slice it parallel to the axis. Take a thin strip of the region, of width $\Delta x$ and height $h$, at distance $r$ from the axis. Spin it around the axis and it sweeps out a thin hollow cylinder: a can with no top or bottom. That's a cylindrical shell (MATH1342 notes p. 6).

Cut the can down one side and unroll it. You get a flat box that is $2\pi r$ long (the circumference), $h$ tall and $\Delta x$ thick. So one shell has volume about $2\pi r\cdot h\cdot\Delta x$, and adding up the shells gives $V=\int_a^b2\pi r\,h\,dx$.

What to notice: the arch $y=x-x^2$ (notes p. 5) spins around the $y$-axis. The thin strip at $x$ is parallel to the axis, $r=x$ away from it, and $h=x-x^2$ tall. Spun, it becomes a shell; unrolled, a box. Notice that the box's length is the circumference, not the radius.
An everyday version

A roll of paper towels is made of thin cylindrical layers of paper. Unroll one layer: it's a long thin sheet, $2\pi r$ long for that layer's radius $r$, as wide as the roll is tall ($h$), and one sheet thick ($\Delta x$). The roll's paper is all those sheets added up. Tree rings and onion layers work the same way.

Three questions to ask every time
  1. Which way do I slice? Parallel to the axis. For a vertical axis ($y$-axis, $x=1$, $x=5$…) that means vertical strips of width $dx$.
  2. What is $r$? The distance from the axis to the strip. For the axis $x=4$ it's $4-x$, not $x$ (the green note on notes p. 6).
  3. What is $h$? The strip's length: the top curve minus the bottom curve, at that $x$.

From shells to an integral (MATH1342 notes p. 6)

$$V_{\text{shell}}\approx2\pi r\,h\,\Delta x,\qquad V\approx\sum_{i=1}^{n}2\pi r_i\,h_i\,\Delta x\ \longrightarrow\ \int_a^b2\pi r\,h\,dx$$

It's the notes p. 34 move (§2.6) a third time: strips of area $f(x_i)\,\Delta x$, then slabs of volume $A(x_i)\,\Delta x$, and now shells of volume $2\pi r_ih_i\,\Delta x$. The only new work is finding the shell's volume.

Why the unrolled piece is a box (and why $\Delta x$ doesn't spoil it)

A shell is a big cylinder of radius $r+\Delta x$ with a smaller cylinder of radius $r$ removed from its middle, both of height $h$. Volumes subtract, exactly as for a washer:

$$\pi(r+\Delta x)^2h-\pi r^2h=\pi h\,(2r\,\Delta x+\Delta x^2)=2\pi\Big(r+\tfrac{\Delta x}{2}\Big)h\,\Delta x$$

That is exactly a box with length $2\pi(r+\frac{\Delta x}2)$, the circumference at the shell's middle, times $h$ times $\Delta x$. Using $2\pi r$ instead is off by $\pi h\,\Delta x^2$, which is tiny next to $\Delta x$ and disappears in the limit. So “cut and unroll” isn't a trick: it's the big-cylinder-minus-small-cylinder idea you already know from washers.

What to notice
  1. With MATH1342 notes p. 5: y = x − x², y-axis, the strip at $x$ has $r=x$ and $h=x-x^2$. The bottom graph shows $2\pi x(x-x^2)$, which peaks at $x=\frac23$ (value $\frac{8\pi}{27}\approx0.93$) and is zero at both ends. The area under it, $\frac\pi6\approx0.5236$, is the volume. Drag the axis to $x=-1$: every $r$ grows by 1 and the volume becomes $\frac\pi2$.
  2. With MATH1342 notes p. 7: above y = 3, axis x = 1, the region is the cap of a parabola above the line $y=3$, from $x=1$ to $x=3$. The strip's height is $4x-x^2-3$: zero at both ends, 1 at $x=2$. The radius $r=x-1$ grows from the left. $V=\frac{8\pi}3\approx8.378$.
  3. Switch to MATH1342 notes p. 7: same region, axis x = 4. Now $r=4-x$ shrinks from 3 to 1 as the strip moves right. $V=\frac{16\pi}3\approx16.755$, exactly twice the axis $x=1$ answer. The region is symmetric about $x=2$, so on average its strips are 1 unit from $x=1$ but 2 units from $x=4$.
  4. With MATH1342 notes p. 8: axis x = 5, the region runs from $x=-2$ to $x=1$ between the line (top) and the parabola (bottom), so $h=-x^2-x+2$ is zero at both ends. $r=5-x$ is between 4 and 7. $V=\frac{99\pi}2\approx155.5$.
  5. With MATH1342 notes p. 9: axis x = −2, the axis is to the left of the region, so $r=x-(-2)=x+2$. The region starts at the point where both curves meet, so $h=0$ at $x=0$ and at $x=2$. $V=\frac{232\pi}{15}\approx48.59$.
  6. Textbook Ex. 3.5.12: y-axis is the curved triangle between $y=2-x$ and $y=\sqrt x$. Shells need one integral. With washers you'd need two, because the region's boundary changes at $y=1$. $V=\frac{8\pi}{15}\approx1.676$ either way.
  7. Raise $n$ for any preset. The stack of shells, $\sum2\pi r_ih_i\,\Delta x$ with right endpoints, approaches the exact volume. Slide the axis into the region and the explorer refuses: strips on both sides of the axis would overlap.
The region above $y=3$ and below $y=4x-x^2$ ($1\le x\le3$) is spun about the line $x=4$. What is the radius $r$ of the shell made by the strip at $x$?
That's the distance from the $y$-axis. The axis here is $x=4$, so the strip at $x$ is $4-x$ away from it. (Watch for this one in Practice 2: for this particular region the wrong $r$ can still produce the right total.)
The strip is to the left of the axis, so the distance is the larger minus the smaller: $4-x$. Then $V=\int_1^32\pi(4-x)(4x-x^2-3)\,dx=\frac{16\pi}3\approx16.755$.
That's negative on $[1,3]$, so you'd get $V=-\frac{16\pi}3$. A radius is a distance: always positive.
That's the distance from the axis to the $y$-axis, a constant. $r$ changes from strip to strip: it's the distance from the axis to that strip.
Spin the same region (above $y=3$, below $y=4x-x^2$) about $x=1$ and about $x=4$. Which solid is bigger?
The axis $x=1$ is at the region's left edge, so its strips are 0 to 2 units away, 1 on average. The solid is $\frac{8\pi}3\approx8.378$, the smaller one.
The axis $x=4$ is 1 unit beyond the region's right edge, so its strips are 1 to 3 units away, 2 on average: twice as far, so shells with twice the circumference. $V=\frac{16\pi}3\approx16.755=2\cdot\frac{8\pi}3$.
Same region, different axes: not the same solid. The farther the strips are from the axis, the longer each shell's circumference $2\pi r$.
Region A lies under $y=x-x^2$ and above the $x$-axis. Region B lies between $y=x$ and $y=x^2$. Both have $0\le x\le1$, and both are spun about the $y$-axis. Which solid is bigger?
Compare the strips. At each $x$, A's strip runs from $0$ to $x-x^2$, so $h=x-x^2$.
At each $x$, B's strip runs from $x^2$ to $x$, so $h=x-x^2$. That's the same as A's.
At every $x$ both strips have the same distance to the axis ($r=x$) and the same height ($h=x-x^2$). Shells don't care where a strip sits vertically, only how long it is. Both volumes are $\frac\pi6$: A by notes p. 5, B by the washer method in the previous topic (Practice 5 there).

Guided example, then your turn

Guided example

Let $S$ be the region bounded by $y=x-x^2$ and $y=0$. Find the volume of the solid obtained by rotating $S$ about the $y$-axis. This is MATH1342 notes p. 5. Work it as the notes do.

Step 1 · Slice
How do you slice for the shell method?
The notes' rule: “slice parallel to axis of rotation.” Each strip at position $x$ spins into a thin shell around the $y$-axis.
Step 2 · Limits

Set $x-x^2=0$, so $x(1-x)=0$. The region runs from $x=0$ to $x=$

$x=0$ or $x=1$. These are the $x$-limits of the integral, because the strips are labeled by $x$.
Step 3 · Radius

The axis is the $y$-axis ($x=0$), so the strip at $x$ is $r=x-0=x$ away.
Step 4 · Height

The strip runs from $y=0$ up to $y=x-x^2$, so $h=(x-x^2)-0=x-x^2$ (the notes write the “$-0$”).
Step 5 · One shell

$2\pi rh\,\Delta x=2\pi x(x-x^2)\,\Delta x$.
Step 6 · Integrate

$2\pi\int_0^1(x^2-x^3)\,dx=2\pi\big[\frac{x^3}3-\frac{x^4}4\big]_0^1=2\pi\big(\frac13-\frac14\big)=\frac\pi6\approx0.5236$. The notes write it as $\big(\frac23\pi x^3-\frac12\pi x^4\big)\Big|_0^1=\frac\pi6$ (verified by hand, SymPy and Wolfram).
Step 7 · Sanity check
The arch peaks at height $\frac14$ (at $x=\frac12$) and spans $x\le1$, so the solid fits inside a cylinder of radius 1 and height $\frac14$. Does $\frac\pi6$ fit?
A cheap plausibility check: the answer must be smaller than the volume of a simple shape that contains the solid. It also rules out the common slips: $\frac{2\pi}{3}\approx2.09$ (using $h=x$) is bigger than $\frac\pi4$, so it can't be right.
Takeaway. Slice parallel to the axis. $r$ is the distance from the axis to the strip, $h$ is the strip's length (top minus bottom), and the shell's volume is circumference × height × thickness, $2\pi rh\,\Delta x$. Integrate over the region's $x$-range.

Tempting approaches vs. correct reasoning

✗ Tempting
“The radius of a shell is $x$,” so for the region above $y=3$ and below $y=4x-x^2$ spun about $x=1$: $V=2\pi\int_1^3x(4x-x^2-3)\,dx=\frac{16\pi}{3}$.
✓ Correct
$r$ is the distance to the axis, which is $x=1$: $r=x-1$. So $V=2\pi\int_1^3(x-1)(4x-x^2-3)\,dx=\frac{8\pi}3$ (MATH1342 notes p. 7).

Common mistakes: why they happen and how to prevent them

Measuring $r$ from the $y$-axis when the axis is $x=k$

Why it happens
The first example (notes p. 5) uses the $y$-axis, where $r=x$. It's easy to carry that over to every problem.

Prevent it
Draw the axis, then write $r$ as a distance to it: $x-1$ for $x=1$, $4-x$ for $x=4$, $5-x$ for $x=5$, $x+2$ for $x=-2$.

A radius that is negative

Why it happens
Writing “$x-4$” for the axis $x=4$ without checking which side the strip is on.

Prevent it
A radius is a distance, so it's positive. For a region to the left of the axis use “axis − $x$”; to the right, “$x$ − axis”. A negative volume is the red flag.

A right answer from a wrong set-up

Why it happens
For the notes p. 7 region about $x=4$, the wrong radius $r=x$ gives $\frac{16\pi}3$, which is also the right answer. That's luck: the region is symmetric about $x=2$, and the $y$-axis and $x=4$ are mirror images in that line.

Prevent it
Check $r$ and $h$ themselves, not just the final number. The “set up only” instruction on notes pp. 7–9 means the set-up is what's graded.

Using only the top curve for $h$

Why it happens
On notes p. 5 the region sits on the $x$-axis, so $h$ is just the top curve. It isn't on pp. 7–9.

Prevent it
Always write $h$ as top minus bottom: $(4x-x^2)-3$, $(-x+3)-(x^2+1)$, $(\sqrt{8x}+1)-(x^2+1)$.

Dropping the $2\pi$ (or using $\pi r^2h$)

Why it happens
The washer formula starts with $\pi$, and $\pi r^2h$ is the volume of a solid cylinder.

Prevent it
A shell is hollow: unrolled, it's a box of length $2\pi r$, height $h$ and thickness $\Delta x$.

Using washers with $dx$ for a vertical axis

Why it happens
$\pi(R^2-r^2)\,dx$ was the first formula you learned for volumes.

Prevent it
Ask whether the strip is parallel or perpendicular to the axis. Washers need a strip perpendicular to it, which for a vertical axis means integrating in $y$.

Taking the $y$-values of the intersection points as the limits

Why it happens
Solving $x^2+1=\sqrt{8x}+1$ gives $x=0$ and $x=2$. Then you compute the $y$-values, which are 1 and 5, and it's easy to grab the wrong pair.

Prevent it
The integral is in $dx$, so the limits are $x$-values. (Notes p. 9's own work slips here: it writes $x=0\to y=0$, but $y=0^2+1=1$. The limits it uses are right.)

An axis that cuts through the region

Why it happens
The formula $r=|x-\text{axis}|$ looks like it should work anywhere.

Prevent it
If the axis passes through the region, strips on the two sides sweep out overlapping shells, and adding them counts the overlap twice. Every example in your notes has the axis outside the region (or touching it).

Stage 6 · Practice

Practice: standard applications

The professor's examples say “set up only”. Most problems here ask for $r$ and $h$ (the set-up) and then for the volume, so you can check the integral by evaluating it.

Let $S$ be the region between $y=4x-x^2$ and $y=3$. Find the volume of the solid obtained by rotating $S$ about the line $x=1$. The notes stop at the integral; write $r$ and $h$, and then evaluate it.
The curves meet where $4x-x^2=3$, at $x=1$ and $x=3$. The parabola is on top (it reaches 4 at $x=2$).
The axis $x=1$ is at the left edge of the region, so the strip at $x$ is $x-1$ away.
$V=\int_1^32\pi(x-1)(4x-x^2-3)\,dx$. With $w=x-1$, $4x-x^2-3=w(2-w)$, so $V=2\pi\int_0^2w^2(2-w)\,dw=2\pi\big(\frac{16}3-4\big)=\frac{8\pi}3\approx8.378$. A check by washers in $y$ (slice horizontally for $3\le y\le4$): $V=4\pi\int_3^4\sqrt{4-y}\,dy=\frac{8\pi}3$ ✓.
The same region, between $y=4x-x^2$ and $y=3$, is rotated about the line $x=4$. Find $r$ and the volume.
The region is to the left of $x=4$, so the distance from the axis to the strip is the larger minus the smaller.
$h=4x-x^2-3$ again, and the limits are still 1 and 3.

$V=\int_1^32\pi(4-x)(4x-x^2-3)\,dx$. With $w=x-1$: $4-x=3-w$ and $4x-x^2-3=w(2-w)$, so $V=2\pi\int_0^2(3-w)(2w-w^2)\,dw=2\pi\int_0^2\big(6w-5w^2+w^3\big)\,dw=2\pi\big(12-\frac{40}3+4\big)=2\pi\cdot\frac83=\frac{16\pi}3\approx16.755$.

Notice two things. It's exactly twice the axis-$x=1$ answer ($\frac{8\pi}3$), because the strips are twice as far away on average. And the wrong radius $r=x$ also gives $\frac{16\pi}3$ here, by symmetry about $x=2$. That's luck, which is why this problem asks for $r$ itself.

Let $S$ be the region between $y=x^2+1$ and $y=-x+3$. Find the volume of the solid obtained by rotating $S$ about the line $x=5$.
Set $x^2+1=-x+3$, so $x^2+x-2=0$ and $(x-1)(x+2)=0$.
At $x=0$ the line is at 3 and the parabola at 1, so the line is on top. The region is left of $x=5$, so $r=5-x$.
$V=\int_{-2}^{1}2\pi(5-x)(-x^2-x+2)\,dx=2\pi\big(5\cdot\frac92+\frac94\big)=2\pi\cdot\frac{99}4=\frac{99\pi}2\approx155.51$. (Here $\int_{-2}^1(-x^2-x+2)\,dx=\frac92$ and $\int_{-2}^1x(-x^2-x+2)\,dx=-\frac94$.) The intersection points are $(-2,5)$ and $(1,2)$.
Let $S$ be the region between $y=x^2+1$ and $y=\sqrt{8x}+1$. Find the volume of the solid obtained by rotating $S$ about the line $x=-2$.
The curves meet where $x^2=\sqrt{8x}$, so $x^4=8x$ and $x(x^3-8)=0$: $x=0$ or $x=2$.
On $(0,2)$, $\sqrt{8x}>x^2$ (try $x=1$: $2.83>1$), so the square-root curve is on top. The $+1$ cancels in $h$.

$V=\int_0^22\pi(x+2)\big(\sqrt{8x}-x^2\big)\,dx=2\pi\big(\frac{12}5+2\cdot\frac83\big)=2\pi\cdot\frac{116}{15}=\frac{232\pi}{15}\approx48.59$. (Here $\int_0^2(\sqrt{8x}-x^2)\,dx=\frac83$ and $\int_0^2x(\sqrt{8x}-x^2)\,dx=\frac{12}5$.)

$\int_0^\pi x\sin x\,dx=\big[-x\cos x+\sin x\big]_0^\pi=\pi$ (with $u=x$, $dv=\sin x\,dx$), so $V=2\pi\cdot\pi=2\pi^2\approx19.74$. Washers would need $x$ as a function of $y$, with two branches ($\pi-\sin^{-1}y$ and $\sin^{-1}y$).

$r=x$, so $V=2\pi\int_0^1x\big(1-x^{1/4}\big)\,dx$.

There's an inside/derivative pair: $u=x^2$, $du=2x\,dx$ (notes pp. 3–6). The limits become $0$ and $\pi$.

Set $-x^2+7x=x^2+3$: $2x^2-7x+3=0$, so $(2x-1)(x-3)=0$.
The parabola $-x^2+7x$ is on top between the intersection points. $h=-2x^2+7x-3$ and $r=x$.
$V=2\pi\int_{1/2}^{3}x\big(-2x^2+7x-3\big)\,dx=2\pi\Big[-\frac{x^4}2+\frac{7x^3}3-\frac{3x^2}2\Big]_{1/2}^{3}=2\pi\Big(9+\frac{11}{96}\Big)=\frac{875\pi}{48}\approx57.27$.
Let $B$ be the region bounded by $y=4x-x^2$ and $y=3$ (the region of notes p. 7). Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving $B$ about the line $x=5$.
The region is left of $x=5$, so $r=5-x$. The height is $4x-x^2-3$ and the limits are 1 and 3.
$V=2\pi\int_1^3(5-x)(4x-x^2-3)\,dx=2\pi\int_0^2(4-w)\,w(2-w)\,dw=2\pi\big(16-16+4\big)=8\pi\approx25.13$. The pattern: axes $x=1$, $x=4$, $x=5$ give $\frac{8\pi}3$, $\frac{16\pi}3$, $8\pi$. Measured from the region's middle line $x=2$, the axes are 1, 2 and 3 units away, and the volume is $\frac{8\pi}{3}$ per unit of distance.
Let $S$ be the region between the curves $y=3x-x^2$ and $y=x$. Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving $S$ about the line $x=4$. (The textbook asks only for the set-up.)
The curves meet where $3x-x^2=x$, so $x(2-x)=0$: $x=0$ and $x=2$. The parabola is on top.
$V=2\pi\int_0^2(4-x)(2x-x^2)\,dx=2\pi\int_0^2\big(8x-6x^2+x^3\big)\,dx=2\pi\big[4x^2-2x^3+\frac{x^4}4\big]_0^2=2\pi\cdot4=8\pi\approx25.13$.

Explain why (Level 7)

Why is the volume of a thin shell about $2\pi r\,h\,\Delta x$? And why does your professor use shells for a vertical axis but washers for a horizontal one?

A model explanation

Take a thin vertical strip of the region at distance $r$ from the axis, of height $h$ and width $\Delta x$, and spin it around the axis. It sweeps out a thin hollow cylinder. Cut it down one side and unroll it: it's nearly a box with length $2\pi r$ (the circumference), height $h$ and thickness $\Delta x$, so its volume is about $2\pi r\,h\,\Delta x$ (MATH1342 notes p. 6). Exactly, it's a big cylinder minus a small one, $\pi(r+\Delta x)^2h-\pi r^2h=2\pi(r+\frac{\Delta x}2)h\,\Delta x$, so the error vanishes as $\Delta x\to0$. Adding the shells gives $\int_a^b2\pi rh\,dx$. As for the pairing: the professor integrates in $x$ throughout, so the strip at $x$ is vertical. If the axis is vertical, that strip is parallel to it, so it makes a shell. If the axis is horizontal, the strip is perpendicular to it, so it makes a washer. That's a pattern in the notes rather than a stated rule: either method can handle either axis, but then you'd integrate in $y$.
