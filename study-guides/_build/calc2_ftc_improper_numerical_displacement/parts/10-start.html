How the fourteen topics connect

Left: the techniques for finding antiderivatives (§1.1, §1.3). Each one is a derivative rule run backwards. Middle: the FTC (§2.4), which turns an antiderivative into a definite integral. Right: what to do when the FTC's conditions fail (§2.5 improper, §2.6 numerical), and how it applies to motion on a line (§3.1) and in space (§3.3). At the bottom right are three geometric applications: the length of a curve (§3.3 arc length), and volume by slicing in two ways (§3.5): washers, slicing perpendicular to the axis, and cylindrical shells, slicing parallel to it. Bottom left: three physical applications of the same slice-and-add idea: mass from density (§3.7), the center of mass (§3.8), and work (§3.9). Dashed boxes are prerequisites from earlier material.
