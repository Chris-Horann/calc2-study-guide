Mixed practice: no labels

Practice Levels 5–6: decide the method yourself (CLAUDE.md §14). All fourteen topics, interleaved on purpose.
How this works

Each problem first asks what kind of problem it is. The answer box opens only after you commit to a method, because choosing the method is the skill being practiced. Your first choice counts toward Level 5 in your mastery report.

Find $\displaystyle\int\tan\theta\,d\theta.$
What kind of problem is this?
$\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. With $u=\cos\theta$, $du=-\sin\theta\,d\theta$.
$-\int\frac{du}{u}=-\ln|\cos\theta|+C$ (notes p. 21). The same thing is $\ln|\sec\theta|+C$.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\ln7}\left(3e^x+2\right)dx.$
What kind of problem is this?
An antiderivative is $3e^x+2x$, and $e^{\ln7}=7$.
$\big(21+2\ln7\big)-\big(3+0\big)=18+2\ln7\approx21.892$ (textbook §2.4 Additional Ex. 9).
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{\ln x}{x^3}\,dx.$
What kind of problem is this?
$u=\ln x$, $dv=x^{-3}\,dx$. Then $du=\frac{dx}{x}$ and $v=-\frac{1}{2x^2}$.
$-\frac{\ln x}{2x^2}+\int\frac{1}{2x^3}\,dx=-\frac{\ln x}{2x^2}-\frac{1}{4x^2}+C$.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt[3]{x}}$ or show that it diverges.
What kind of problem is this?
Split: $\lim_{b\to0^-}\int_{-1}^b+\lim_{a\to0^+}\int_a^1$. An antiderivative of $x^{-1/3}$ is $\tfrac32x^{2/3}$.
The left piece is $0-\tfrac32(-1)^{2/3}=-\tfrac32$ and the right piece is $\tfrac32-0=\tfrac32$. Do both converge?
Both pieces converge ($-\tfrac32$ and $\tfrac32$), so the integral converges to 0. Symmetry is legitimate here because each half converges on its own. Contrast $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\,dx$ in §2.5.
Find the length of the curve $y=\tfrac13(x^2+2)^{3/2}$ for $0\le x\le3$.
What kind of problem is this?
$f'(x)=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac32(x^2+2)^{1/2}\cdot2x=x(x^2+2)^{1/2}$.
$1+f'^2=1+x^2(x^2+2)=x^4+2x^2+1=(x^2+1)^2$.
$\int_0^3(x^2+1)\,dx=9+3=12$.
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{2x^2}{x^2-9}\,dx.$
What kind of problem is this?
$\frac{2x^2}{x^2-9}=2+\frac{18}{x^2-9}$.
$\frac{18}{(x-3)(x+3)}=\frac{3}{x-3}-\frac{3}{x+3}$.
$2x+3\ln|x-3|-3\ln|x+3|+C$ (the red “Final answer” on notes p. 23).
Estimate $\displaystyle\int_0^2\sqrt{1+x^3}\,dx$ using the Trapezoid Rule with $n=4$.
Before you start: why is a numerical rule the right tool here?
$\Delta x=\tfrac12$, the points are $0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2$, and the heights are $1,\ \sqrt{1.125},\ \sqrt2,\ \sqrt{4.375},\ 3$.
$\tfrac14\big[1+2(1.06066+1.41421+2.09165)+3\big]\approx3.2833$. (A high-accuracy value is 3.2413. The curve is concave up, so the trapezoids overestimate.)
A particle in the plane has velocity $\vec v(t)=(t-1,\ 0)$ for $0\le t\le3$. Find the total distance traveled.
What does the speed become here?
$|\vec v|=\sqrt{(t-1)^2}=|t-1|$.
$\int_0^1(1-t)\,dt+\int_1^3(t-1)\,dt=\tfrac12+2=\tfrac52$. When motion in the plane is really along a line, $\sqrt{v^2}$ turns back into §3.1's $|v|$.
An object has velocity $v(t)=t-2$ m/s for $0\le t\le5$. How far does it travel?
What does “how far does it travel” call for?
$v=0$ at $t=2$. Compute $\int_0^2(2-t)\,dt$ and $\int_2^5(t-2)\,dt$.
$2+\tfrac92=\tfrac{13}{2}=6.5$ m (displacement: $\tfrac52$ m).
The region under $y=\sqrt x$, $0\le x\le4$, is spun about the $x$-axis. Find the volume of the solid.
What do the slices look like?
$A(x)=\pi(\sqrt x)^2=\pi x$.
$\pi\int_0^4x\,dx=8\pi\approx25.13$.
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{2x+1}{x^2+x+5}\,dx.$
It's a rational function. What's the quickest method?
$\frac{d}{dx}(x^2+x+5)=2x+1$.
$\int\frac{du}{u}=\ln|u|+C=\ln(x^2+x+5)+C$. The absolute value isn't needed here, because $x^2+x+5>0$.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx$ or show that it diverges.
What kind of problem is this?
Use $\lim_{b\to1^-}\int_0^b$. Substituting $u=1-x^2$ gives the antiderivative $-\sqrt{1-x^2}$.
$\lim_{b\to1^-}\left(-\sqrt{1-b^2}\right)-\left(-1\right)=0+1=1.$ It converges.
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{e^{1/x}}{x^3}\,dx.$
What's the plan?
$w=\frac1x$, $dw=-\frac{dx}{x^2}$, and $\frac{1}{x^3}\,dx=\frac1x\cdot\frac{dx}{x^2}=-w\,dw$.
$-\int we^w\,dw=-(we^w-e^w)$.
$-we^w+e^w+C=-\frac1xe^{1/x}+e^{1/x}+C$ (notes p. 24).
For the curve $y=\cos x$, $0\le x\le\pi$: set up an integral for its arc length, and estimate it with the Trapezoid Rule, $n=4$.
Once it's set up, why estimate instead of computing it exactly?
$y'=-\sin x$, so the length is $\int_0^\pi\sqrt{1+\sin^2x}\,dx$.
$\Delta x=\frac\pi4$. The heights at $0,\frac\pi4,\frac\pi2,\frac{3\pi}4,\pi$ are $1,\ \sqrt{1.5},\ \sqrt2,\ \sqrt{1.5},\ 1$.
$\frac\pi8\big[1+2\sqrt{1.5}+2\sqrt2+2\sqrt{1.5}+1\big]=\frac\pi4\big(1+\sqrt2+\sqrt6\big)\approx3.8199$. The actual length is about 3.8202, exactly half of the length of $y=\sin x$ on $[0,2\pi]$ (3.3–3.5 notes p. 4). That makes sense: $\cos x$ on $[0,\pi]$ is half a period of the same wave.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}|\cos x|\,dx.$
What kind of problem is this?
$\cos x<0$ on $(\tfrac\pi2,\pi)$. Split at $\tfrac\pi2$.
$\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos x\,dx+\int_{\pi/2}^{\pi}(-\cos x)\,dx=1+1=2.$
Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{x+5}{x^2-x-2}\,dx.$
What kind of problem is this?
$\frac{x+5}{(x-2)(x+1)}=\frac{A}{x-2}+\frac{B}{x+1}$ gives ① $A+B=1$ and ② $A-2B=5$.
① − ②: $3B=-4$, so $B=-\tfrac43$ and $A=\tfrac73$. The integral is $\tfrac73\ln|x-2|-\tfrac43\ln|x+1|+C$.
A car's velocity is recorded every 2 seconds:
$t$ (s)02468
$v$ (ft/s)06101211
Estimate the distance traveled from $t=0$ to $t=8$ using Simpson's Rule.
What's the plan?
$\Delta x=2$ and $n=4$, which is even ✓. The coefficients are 1, 4, 2, 4, 1 and the prefactor is $\frac23$.
$\frac23\big[0+4(6)+2(10)+4(12)+11\big]=\frac23\cdot103=\frac{206}{3}\approx68.67$ ft. (The Trapezoid Rule gives 67 ft.)
The region between $y=3x-x^2$ and $y=x$ is spun about the line $y=-2$. Find the volume. (Textbook §3.5 Suggested Ex. 7(b) asks only for the setup; finish it here.)
Which setup is right?
The curves meet where $3x-x^2=x$, at $x=0$ and $x=2$. The parabola is on top.
$(3x-x^2+2)^2-(x+2)^2=x^4-6x^3+4x^2+8x$.
$\pi\big[\frac{x^5}5-\frac{3x^4}2+\frac{4x^3}3+4x^2\big]_0^2=\pi\big(\frac{32}5-24+\frac{32}3+16\big)=\frac{136\pi}{15}\approx28.48$.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\ln3}e^x\,(e^x-1)^5\,dx.$
What kind of problem is this?
$u=e^x-1$, $du=e^x\,dx$. Limits: $x=0\to u=0$ and $x=\ln3\to u=2$.
$\int_0^2u^5\,du=\frac{2^6}{6}=\frac{32}{3}$. Notes p. 23 finds the antiderivative $\frac{(e^x-1)^6}{6}$ first and uses the $x$-limits, with the same answer.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^\infty\frac{\ln x}{x}\,dx$ or show that it diverges.
What kind of problem is this?
Substitute $u=\ln x$: an antiderivative is $\frac{(\ln x)^2}{2}$.
$\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{(\ln b)^2}{2}=\infty$, so it diverges.
A particle has position $\vec p(t)=(t,\ t+t^3)$ for $0\le t\le2$. Set up the integral for the total distance traveled and estimate it with Simpson's Rule, $n=4$. (This is textbook §3.3 Suggested Ex. 8, rewritten as motion.)
Once you have the speed, why estimate instead of computing exactly?
$\vec v=(1,\ 1+3t^2)$, so the distance is $\int_0^2\sqrt{1+(1+3t^2)^2}\,dt$.
$\Delta t=\tfrac12$ at $t=0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2$, with Simpson coefficients $1,4,2,4,1$. The heights are $\sqrt2,\ \sqrt{4.0625},\ \sqrt{17},\ \sqrt{61.0625},\ \sqrt{170}$.
$\frac{1/2}{3}\big[\sqrt2+4\sqrt{4.0625}+2\sqrt{17}+4\sqrt{61.0625}+\sqrt{170}\big]\approx10.3363$. A high-accuracy value is about 10.3403. As a sanity check, the displacement $(2,10)$ has length $\sqrt{104}\approx10.198$, which is less than the distance ✓. The same integral is the arc length of the graph $y=x+x^3$ on $[0,2]$ (§3.3 arc length): the particle's path is that graph.
An object starts at $p(0)=4$ m with $v(t)=3-t$ m/s for $0\le t\le6$. Find (a) its position at $t=6$ and (b) its total distance traveled.
Which integrals do you need?
$\int_0^6(3-t)\,dt=\big[3t-\tfrac{t^2}{2}\big]_0^6=18-18=0$.
For distance, $v=0$ at $t=3$: $\int_0^3(3-t)\,dt=4.5$ and $\int_3^6(t-3)\,dt=4.5$.
(a) $p(6)=4+0=4$ m: it's back where it started. (b) $4.5+4.5=9$ m.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\,e^{-x^2}\,dx$ or show that it diverges.
What's the correct first move?
An antiderivative is $-\tfrac12e^{-x^2}$. Compute $\int_0^\infty$ and $\int_{-\infty}^0$ separately.
$\int_0^\infty xe^{-x^2}\,dx=\tfrac12$ and $\int_{-\infty}^0xe^{-x^2}\,dx=-\tfrac12$. Both converge, so the total is $0$.
The region under $y=e^{-x}$ for $x\ge0$ is spun about the $x$-axis. Find the volume of the solid, or show that it's infinite.
What's the setup?
$A(x)=\pi\big(e^{-x}\big)^2=\pi e^{-2x}$.
$\pi\lim_{b\to\infty}\big[-\tfrac12e^{-2x}\big]_0^b$.
$\pi\big(0+\tfrac12\big)=\frac\pi2\approx1.571$ (textbook §3.5 Suggested Ex. 9).
Is $\displaystyle\int_0^1e^{-x^2}\,dx$ improper? Approximate it with Simpson's Rule, $n=2$.
Classify it.
$\Delta x=\tfrac12$, the points are $0,\tfrac12,1$, and $S_2=\tfrac16\big[1+4e^{-1/4}+e^{-1}\big]$.
$\approx0.747180$ (textbook Example 2.6.7). Its error is only about $3.6\times10^{-4}$.
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^2\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$ or show that it diverges.
What kind of problem is this?
An antiderivative is $\sin^{-1}\frac x2$ (notes p. 1). Use $\lim_{b\to2^-}$.
$\lim_{b\to2^-}\sin^{-1}\frac b2-0=\frac\pi2.$ It converges.
The region between $y=x^2$ and $y=x+2$ is spun about the line $x=4$. Find the volume of the solid.
Which setup is right?
The curves meet where $x^2=x+2$, so $(x-2)(x+1)=0$: $x=-1$ and $x=2$. The line is on top between them.
$h=(x+2)-x^2$ and $r=4-x$. Expand: $(4-x)(2+x-x^2)=x^3-5x^2+2x+8$.
$V=2\pi\int_{-1}^{2}\big(x^3-5x^2+2x+8\big)\,dx=2\pi\Big[\frac{x^4}4-\frac{5x^3}3+x^2+8x\Big]_{-1}^{2}=2\pi\Big(\frac{32}3+\frac{61}{12}\Big)=\frac{63\pi}2\approx98.96$.
The same region, between $y=x^2$ and $y=x+2$, is now spun about the line $y=-1$. Find the volume of the solid.
Which setup is right?
The region is the same as in Mixed 27, but the axis is horizontal this time. The curves still meet at $x=-1$ and $x=2$.
The axis is below the region. $R=(x+2)-(-1)=x+3$ and $r=x^2-(-1)=x^2+1$.
$V=\pi\int_{-1}^{2}\big(-x^4-x^2+6x+8\big)\,dx=\pi\Big[-\frac{x^5}5-\frac{x^3}3+3x^2+8x\Big]_{-1}^{2}=\pi\Big(\frac{284}{15}+\frac{67}{15}\Big)=\frac{117\pi}5\approx73.51$. One region, two axes, two methods: shells for the vertical axis (Mixed 27), washers for the horizontal one.
The region under $y=e^{x^2}$ for $0\le x\le1$ is spun about the $y$-axis. Find the volume of the solid.
What kind of problem is this?
$r=x$ and $h=e^{x^2}$, so $V=2\pi\int_0^1x\,e^{x^2}\,dx$.
$u=x^2$, $du=2x\,dx$. The limits $0$ and $1$ stay $0$ and $1$.
$2\pi\int_0^1x\,e^{x^2}\,dx=\pi\int_0^1e^u\,du=\pi(e-1)\approx5.398$. A shell is the natural choice: washers would need $x=\sqrt{\ln y}$, split into the pieces $0\le y\le1$ and $1\le y\le e$.
A thin rod lies along $1\le x\le4$ (metres) with density $\delta(x)=\sqrt x$ kg/m. Find its mass.
What kind of problem is this?
$\int\sqrt x\,dx=\frac23x^{3/2}+C$.
$M=\frac23x^{3/2}\Big|_1^4=\frac23(8-1)=\frac{14}3\approx4.667$ kg.
Where does the rod of Mixed 30 ($1\le x\le4$, density $\sqrt x$ kg/m) balance?
What do you compute?
$M=\frac{14}3$ (Mixed 30). $\bar xM=\int_1^4x^{3/2}\,dx=\frac25x^{5/2}\Big|_1^4$.
$\bar xM=\frac25(32-1)=\frac{62}5$, so $\bar x=\frac{62}5\cdot\frac3{14}=\frac{93}{35}\approx2.657$ m. (The mass midpoint is $4.5^{2/3}\approx2.726$, a different point.)
A cylindrical tank of radius 2 m and height 5 m is full of water (density 1000 kg/m³). Find the work needed to pump all the water out over the top.
Which set-up is right?
Every slice is a disk of radius 2: weight $1000\cdot9.8\cdot4\pi\,\Delta y=39{,}200\pi\,\Delta y$ N.
$W=39{,}200\pi\int_0^5(5-y)\,dy=39{,}200\pi\cdot12.5=490{,}000\pi\approx1.54\times10^6$ J. Check with §3.8: the water weighs $39{,}200\pi\cdot5$ N and its center of mass rises 2.5 m.
A thin plate occupies the region between $y=\sqrt x$ and $y=x^2$, with area density $\delta(x)=x$. Find its mass.
Which integral is it?
$\sqrt x=x^2$ at $x=0$ and $x=1$. At $x=\frac14$: $\sqrt x=0.5$ and $x^2=0.0625$.
$M=\int_0^1\big(x^{3/2}-x^3\big)\,dx=\frac25-\frac14=\frac3{20}$. Same integrand as the shell volume about the $y$-axis, without the $2\pi$: a density of $x$ weights each strip by its distance from the $y$-axis, just like a shell's radius does.
It takes 6 J of work to stretch a spring 0.2 m beyond its natural length. How much work does it take to stretch it from 0.2 m to 0.4 m beyond its natural length?
What's the plan?
$\frac12k(0.2)^2=6$, so $k=300$ N/m.
$W=\frac12\cdot300\big(0.4^2-0.2^2\big)=150\cdot0.12=18$ J. Doubling the stretch quadruples the work (24 J from 0), so the second 0.2 m costs three times the first.
A square plate occupies $0\le x\le2$, $0\le y\le2$, with area density $\delta(x)=x$. Find its center of mass $(\bar x,\bar y)$.
Which coordinates need an integral?
$dm=x\cdot(2-0)\,dx=2x\,dx$, so $M=\int_0^22x\,dx=4$.
$\bar xM=\int_0^22x^2\,dx=\frac{16}3$, so $\bar x=\frac43$. Each strip's midpoint is $\frac{2+0}2=1$, so $\bar y=1$ (check: $\bar yM=\int_0^21\cdot2x\,dx=4$).
